## Step 1. Analyze the Story

Analyze the story first.

Provide the analysis in **Korean** for reviewer verification.

Include the following sections:

- 등장인물 (Characters)
- 배경 (Setting)
- 주요 사건 (Major Events)
- 등장인물의 목표 (Character Goals)
- 등장인물의 감정 변화 (Character Feelings)
- 갈등 (Conflict)
- 해결 (Resolution)
- 주제 (Theme)
- 대화 화자 / 주인공 (Speaking Main Character)
- 핵심 전이 주제 (Transfer Anchors)

## Speaking Main Character

Identify the single character who speaks directly to the learner in first person.

This character uses "I", "me", and "my" when referring to themselves in the Opening Line and in all Recall and Inference questions.

Required:
- Name exactly one Speaking Main Character.
- Confirm that this character is the perspective through which Recall and Inference questions will be framed.

All Recall and Inference questions about this character must use first-person perspective:
- "What did I...?" not "What did [Name]...?"
- "Why was I...?" not "Why was [Name]...?"
- "How did I feel...?" not "How did [Name] feel...?"

## Transfer Anchors

Identify 2–4 core themes from the story that connect naturally to the learner's real life.

Find Transfer Anchors in:
- the main conflict
- the main character's main problem
- an important action or decision
- a meaningful character change
- the resolution
- the core lesson or message

Do not use incidental nouns, settings, or minor characters as Transfer Anchors.

### Poppy — Example

Story core:
- Poppy only eats one kind of food.
- He is afraid or unwilling to try other foods.
- He finally tries new food and finds it good.
- His attitude toward new things changes.

Good Transfer Anchors:
- trying new food
- trying something new you were afraid of
- changing your mind after trying something
- being open to new experiences

Bad Transfer Anchors:
- rabbits (Chef Pip is a rabbit — incidental character detail)
- favorite animals (the story is not about animals generally)
- friends (the picnic companions are incidental to the conflict)
- colors alone (colors appear but are not the central conflict)

### Pip — Example

Story core:
- Pip and Dot grow together and their friendship deepens through change.

Good Transfer Anchors:
- friendship
- changing and growing with a friend
- doing something new

Note: "What do you usually do with your friends?" is a reasonable Transfer question for Pip because friendship is a central theme. The same question is NOT appropriate for Poppy because friendship is incidental to Poppy's story.

### Cat (Puss in Boots) — Example

Story core:
- Cat is clever and makes plans to help his poor master succeed.
- The master gains power and wealth because of Cat's schemes.
- A giant is tricked by Cat and transformed.

Good Transfer Anchors:
- cleverness and planning
- helping others with your skills
- using your strengths to make things better

Bad Transfer Anchors:
- boots (incidental object Cat requested)
- giants (a story character — minor story element, not a learner-connectable theme)
- kings (incidental setting element)

Good Transfer questions:
- The Cat is good at making plans. What are you good at?
- Tell me about a time you helped a friend. What did you do?

---

### Didi — Example

Story core:
- Didi catches a beautiful cloud and tries to own it.
- The cloud eventually escapes and floats away.
- Didi learns that some things cannot and should not be owned.
- A friend (Podo) gives honest advice that Didi initially ignores.

Good Transfer Anchors:
- wanting to own something that isn't yours
- letting beautiful things be free
- learning from your own mistake
- listening to honest advice from a friend

Bad Transfer Anchors:
- clouds (incidental object — not a learner-connectable theme)
- space (setting detail)

Good Transfer questions:
- You really want something, but it is not yours. What do you do?
- Your friend gives you advice, but you think differently. What do you do?
- Has someone given you advice that turned out to be right? What happened?

---

### Hans — Example

Story core:
- Hans trades his gold for a horse, then trades the horse away for progressively lesser things.
- Hans ends up with nothing but feels happy and free.
- The central conflict is whether quick trades bring real happiness or regret.
- The lesson is about knowing what you truly need vs. what others say is better.

Good Transfer Anchors:
- making quick decisions without thinking carefully
- knowing what you really need
- freedom vs. possessions
- when others tell you something is better — is it always true?

Bad Transfer Anchors:
- goose (incidental trade item)
- gold (opening possession — not the lesson itself)
- traveling home (setting detail)

Good Transfer questions:
- What can you do before you decide too quickly?
- How can you know what you really need?
- What makes you feel free and happy?

---

### Kira — Example

Story core:
- Kira and Toby explore the deep ocean and find a fish in danger.
- The anglerfish's lamp is fading because a plastic bag is trapped over her head.
- Kira and Toby use the submarine's robot arms to remove the plastic bag.
- The central conflict is about protecting sea creatures and taking action to help.

Good Transfer Anchors:
- protecting the ocean and sea animals
- taking action to help someone in need
- caring for the environment

Bad Transfer Anchors:
- anglerfish (incidental character — not a learner-connectable theme by itself)
- submarines (setting/tool — not the lesson)
- deep sea (setting detail)

Good Transfer questions:
- What can you do to help keep the sea clean?
- What can you do when an animal needs help?
- How do you feel when you help someone?

---

### Milo — Example

Story core:
- Milo is a chameleon who wakes up gray and cannot change colors.
- He sees all his friends with beautiful colors and feels different.
- Milo eventually discovers and accepts his own special pattern.
- The central conflict is about accepting yourself and valuing what makes you unique.

Good Transfer Anchors:
- accepting yourself
- what makes you special or different
- valuing your own unique qualities

Bad Transfer Anchors:
- colors (surface-level noun — important symbolically, but "What colors do you like?" is not a Transfer question because it does not connect to the lesson)
- chameleons (the animal — not the learner-connectable theme)

Good Transfer questions:
- What makes you special?
- What is different about you and your friends?
- What do you think your color is? Why?

Bad Transfer questions (reason: surface-level, not connected to the lesson of self-acceptance):
- What color do you like best?
- What colors can you find outside?
